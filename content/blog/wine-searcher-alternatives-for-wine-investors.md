# Wine-Searcher Alternatives in 2026: A Guide for Wine Investors

Wine-Searcher has long been the default answer to one specific question: *where can I buy this bottle at the best price?* With roughly 18 million listings from 35,000 stores across 130 countries and around 250 million searches a year (figures the company stated in May 2026), it remains the reference for that job.

In May 2026, however, ownership changed: Wine-Searcher was **acquired by GLX U.S. Inc.**, a New York company wholly owned by Platin Sàrl, the investment vehicle of the family of Olivier Goudet — the French financier, former CFO of Mars and co-founder of JAB Holding. The purchase price was not disclosed. The new owners stated two priorities: expansion in "the critical US market" and "a greater emphasis on the use of AI". According to trade press, this is the second ownership change in under three years.

For consumers, little changes for now. But if your interest in wine is **investment** — building, monitoring and eventually selling a collection — the right question isn't "where is it cheapest", and no single tool covers it. Here is a factual map of the alternatives, by need.

## To buy at auction or sell a collection

- **iDealwine** (France) — an online wine auction house that also sells at fixed prices. Ships to 40+ countries. Sellers pay a 13% commission (plus VAT) on the hammer price; cellar valuations are free.
- **Catawiki** — a general online auction marketplace with an expert-curated wine category and weekly auctions. Useful for single lots and for testing demand.
- **Traditional auction houses** — Christie's and Sotheby's both accept consignments from private collectors with free online estimates (Sotheby's indicates a collection threshold of roughly $20,000). In Italy, Aste Bolaffi, Pandolfini and Wannenes run active wine departments with free photo-based preliminary valuations.

## To trade fine wine

- **BordeauxIndex LiveTrade** (UK) — a trading platform open to private clients with no user fees: the margin is built into prices, which are shown transparently. Requires wine stored in a UK/EU bonded warehouse.
- **Liv-ex** — the B2B fine wine exchange: membership is restricted to wine trade businesses, not private collectors. It is, however, the source of the industry's benchmark indices, which any serious investor should learn to read (liv-ex.com).

## To manage and analyse a collection

- **CellarTracker** — the community standard for cellar inventory and tasting notes.
- **Vivino** — consumer-oriented ratings and prices, less relevant for investment-grade wine.
- **VinoInvest** — our platform: we don't aggregate millions of retail offers; we analyse a curated catalogue of investment-grade wines with an AI Score whose methodology is public, side-by-side comparison tools, watchlists and a structured Academy, with a specific focus on Italy and Europe — including Italian tax treatment for private collectors. We don't provide financial advice: we provide analysis tools, and the decisions stay yours.

## How to choose

A simple rule: **price-comparison engines answer "where to buy"; auction and trading platforms answer "how to sell"; intelligence tools answer "what to buy and when"**. Experienced investors typically use more than one. And with Wine-Searcher's declared centre of gravity shifting towards the United States under its new ownership, investors operating in Europe have one more reason to know the full toolbox.
