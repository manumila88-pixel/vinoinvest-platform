# The Wine-Searcher Acquisition (May 2026): What It Means for European Collectors

In mid-May 2026 the trade press reported that **Wine-Searcher, the world's largest wine price search engine, had been acquired by GLX U.S. Inc.**, a New York-based company wholly owned by Platin Sàrl, the investment vehicle of the family of Olivier Goudet. This article collects the verified facts and the open questions — no speculation.

## The confirmed facts

- **The buyer**: GLX U.S. Inc. / Platin Sàrl. Olivier Goudet is a French financier, former CFO of Mars and co-founder and former CEO of JAB Holding (the group behind brands such as Pret A Manger and Krispy Kreme).
- **The price**: not disclosed.
- **The stated strategy**: Grégory Andre, senior partner at GLX, spoke of providing "the capital and strategic focus necessary to scale the business further, with a particular emphasis on expansion in the critical US market", and of "a greater emphasis on the use of AI".
- **The scale**: at the time of the announcement, Wine-Searcher reported roughly 18 million listings from 35,000 stores in 130 countries and around 250 million searches a year. Headquarters remain in Auckland, New Zealand, with offices in the UK.
- **The context**: this is the second ownership change in under three years — the previous owner, Flaviar Inc., had acquired the company in late 2023, according to trade reports.

## The question the industry is asking

One point raised by the specialist press (notably Vino Joy News) deserves attention: the Goudet family holds roughly 9% of Treasury Wine Estates, one of the world's largest wine groups, and owns Château Charmail in the Haut-Médoc. Wine-Searcher built its reputation on **neutrality** — a data layer with no direct stake in the wine it catalogues. With a commercially active owner in the sector, the press is asking whether that perceived neutrality will remain intact. To be clear: there is no evidence of any change to date. It's an open question, not an accusation.

## What European collectors should watch

1. **Geographic priorities.** The declared emphasis is the US market. That doesn't mean Europe is abandoned — but resources follow priorities. Coverage of European merchants, local data quality and language support are the things to monitor.
2. **Pricing and the paywall.** Free users currently see only part of the listings; the Pro subscription unlocks the rest. New owners eventually review pricing everywhere — nothing has been announced, but that's the place to watch.
3. **What "more AI" actually ships.** It can mean anything from better label matching to new data products. Judge by releases, not announcements.
