# Wine-Searcher vs Wine Investment Platforms: What Investors Actually Need

Comparing Wine-Searcher with a wine investment platform is like comparing a flight aggregator with a travel advisor: different tools for different questions. With Wine-Searcher's acquisition by GLX (May 2026, Goudet family, declared focus on the US market and AI), it's worth restating who does what.

## What Wine-Searcher does — and does well

Wine-Searcher indexes wine retail offers worldwide — roughly 18 million listings from 35,000 stores in 130 countries, per the company's figures from May 2026. You type a label, you see who sells it and for how much. Its main features, as described on the official site:

- **Price search and comparison** across retailers (free users see sponsoring merchants; the full list requires the Pro subscription).
- **Multi-year price history** for Pro subscribers.
- **Price and availability alerts**, limited for free users and expanded for Pro.
- **A label scanner** in the app: photograph a label, get identification and prices.
- **An aggregated critic score** on a 100-point scale.

The business model is merchant-side: stores are indexed for free but pay for visibility (sponsorship, pay-per-click leads, marketplace commissions). For a consumer buying a bottle at the best price, it remains the reference tool.

## What it doesn't do (and doesn't claim to)

Wine-Searcher tells you where to buy and for how much. It doesn't tell you:

- **whether** a wine makes sense in an investment portfolio (diversification, risk, horizon);
- **when** to buy or sell it relative to its reference market;
- **how** to sell a collection (channels, commissions, transport documents, taxes);
- **how much** of a wine's performance comes from vintage, producer or region.

Those are the questions of someone treating wine as an asset — the territory of investment platforms.

## What investment platforms do

Two families exist today:

1. **Managed wine portfolios** (e.g. Cult Wines, Vinovest): you commit capital and the platform builds and stores the portfolio for you, charging annual management fees. Suited to investors who want to delegate.
2. **Self-directed analysis tools** — VinoInvest's family: you keep control of decisions and the platform provides data and tools. In our case: an AI Score with a public methodology over a curated catalogue, side-by-side wine comparison, watchlists, a proprietary index, and a structured Academy — with specific attention to Italian and European tax questions for private collectors. We don't manage money and we don't promise returns: we provide the map, not the destination.

## The honest answer: you need both

A well-equipped wine investor uses different tools at different stages: intelligence to decide *what and when*, a price comparison engine to execute *at the right cost*, an auction or trading venue for the *exit*. No single platform covers the full cycle today — be wary of anyone claiming otherwise.

What the Wine-Searcher ownership change adds is geographic: with expansion declaredly concentrated on the US, investors operating from Europe should make sure at least one of their tools speaks their language — literally, and fiscally.
